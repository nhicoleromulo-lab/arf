Under investigation 01
Corruption · Public Works

The Flood Control Scandal

In 2025, allegations surfaced of substandard, overpriced and “ghost” flood control projects, with kickbacks reportedly shared among officials, contractors and lawmakers. Senate hearings, mass protests and criminal cases followed. Meanwhile, every typhoon season, communities still ask what became of the money meant to protect them.

What testimony & records say

  • Witnesses told the Senate projects were made substandard to cover kickbacks.
  • Charges filed against former lawmakers, officials and contractors; cases continue.
  • 2026 flood control funding was sharply reduced.

Open questions

  • How high does accountability go, and how fast?
  • Are reforms working?
  • Who protects flood-prone families now?
WHAT YOU CAN DO → Check project signboards in your barangay, use transparency portals to look up local works, and report problems to official channels.

Signing expected Nov 2026 02
Economy · Foreign Policy

Pax Silica

A US-led alliance to secure supply chains for chips, AI, critical minerals and advanced manufacturing. The Philippines joined in April 2026, with a roughly 1,600-hectare industrial and tech hub planned in New Clark City, Tarlac. Is it the biggest economic opening in a generation, or a deal whose costs we haven’t counted?

What supporters say

  • Moves us beyond BPO and remittances to higher-value manufacturing.
  • Lets minerals be processed here instead of exported as raw ore.
  • Government says these goals were already underway.

What critics say

  • Fear of mineral extraction, lost sovereignty and being pulled into US–China rivalry.
  • Residents and Aeta communities worry about land and displacement.
  • Economists ask who benefits: distribution is the real issue.
WHAT YOU CAN DO → Read the actual terms once published, follow consultation and environmental requirements, and ask your representatives about jobs, land and taxes.

03 · Sovereignty

West Philippine Sea

The West Philippine Sea is the part of the South China Sea within the Philippines’ exclusive economic zone. It is a fishing ground, a shipping lane and a flashpoint, and for many coastal families it is simply where the day’s catch comes from.

Key points

  • In July 2016, an arbitral tribunal under UNCLOS ruled on the Philippines’ case against China’s claims in the South China Sea.
  • Philippine fishers and coast guard vessels have reported tense encounters at sea in recent years.
  • Disputes affect fish catch, food prices and coastal incomes, not just diplomacy.

Questions to ask

  • What do fishing communities need to work safely?
  • How should the country balance diplomacy, alliances and defense?
  • What does the 2016 ruling mean in practice?

04 · Climate

Typhoons & Floods

The Philippines sits on the typhoon belt and the Pacific Ring of Fire. Storms, floods, landslides and sea-level rise hit the poorest hardest: those in flood plains, coastlines and informal settlements.

Key points

  • PAGASA tracks around 20 tropical cyclones entering the Philippine area each year.
  • Super Typhoon Haiyan (Yolanda) in 2013 killed thousands and displaced millions.
  • Drainage, early warning, land use and honest infrastructure all shape how bad a flood becomes.

Questions to ask

  • Is your barangay flood-ready, with maps, shelters and warnings?
  • How is flood-control spending monitored?
  • Who helps families rebuild after a storm?

05 · Learning

Education Gaps

Access to school has expanded, but learning is another matter. International assessments and national reviews have raised concerns about reading, math and science skills, classroom shortages and teacher workload.

Key points

  • In the 2022 PISA assessment, Filipino 15-year-olds scored well below the international average.
  • A national commission (EDCOM 2) reviewed the education sector and recommended reforms.
  • Skills taught in school and skills employers need do not always match.

Questions to ask

  • Do students in your area have books, classrooms and teachers?
  • Is learning being measured and improved?
  • How do schools prepare students for real jobs?

06 · Economy

Cost of Living

When rice, fuel, rent and electricity rise faster than wages, families trade off: skip a meal, delay a clinic visit, pull a child from school. Price trends are tracked monthly, so you can check them yourself.

Key points

  • The Philippine Statistics Authority publishes inflation and poverty data on a schedule.
  • Food prices weigh heavily on poor households’ budgets.
  • Wages, jobs and prices together decide what money can actually buy.

Questions to ask

  • Are wages keeping up with prices?
  • Which prices hit your household most?
  • What support exists for the poorest families?

07 · Information

Disinformation

Fake posts, edited photos and misleading videos spread fastest when people are angry or afraid, exactly when facts matter most. Checking before sharing is a form of civic duty.

Key points

  • Fact-checkers in the Philippines review viral claims and publish their findings.
  • Old photos from other disasters often circulate as ‘new’ ones.
  • Outrage is a signal to slow down, not to share.

Questions to ask

  • Who made this post and why?
  • Is it recent and from the original source?
  • What do independent fact-checkers say?

08 · Health

Healthcare Access

A good hospital in the city means little if a family in a faraway island barangay can’t reach it. Access depends on facilities, health workers, medicine prices and insurance coverage.

Key points

  • The Universal Health Care Act (RA 11223) aims for coverage for all Filipinos.
  • Many health workers seek jobs abroad, leaving gaps at home.
  • Distance and cost still keep some families from care.

Questions to ask

  • Where is your nearest health center?
  • What does PhilHealth cover for your family?
  • Are medicines affordable and available?
